[8] p4 x72: ## Target Industry: Canadian Logistics

[9] p4 x108: [[The project focuses on logistics in Canada, with particular interest in freight transportation and the digital services that help firms book and coordinate shipments. Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (2018) defines logistics as the planning, implementation, and control of the movement and storage of goods, services, and related information. In business practice, these activities connect suppliers, production sites, warehouses, carriers, and customers. Freight transportation is central to that process, but reliable distribution also depends on inventory control, order processing, and the exchange of accurate information.]]

[10] p4 x108: [[The industry is closely tied to Canada’s domestic economy and international trade.]] Transport Canada (2025b) reports that transportation and warehousing contributed CAD 96.5 billion, or 4.3% of national GDP, in 2024. International merchandise trade was approximately CAD 1.55 trillion that year, by value road transportation carried 46%, marine transport 21.1%, air 11.6%, and rail 10.5%. These figures describe the transportation and warehousing sector and merchandise flows, respectively.

[11] p4 x108: Mordor Intelligence (2026) estimates that freight transportation accounted for 60.88% of the Canadian freight and logistics market in 2025. That scale, combined with the growing use of digital freight marketplaces, makes this field a relevant starting point for examining employers and the qualifications they seek. [[Freightera, a Vancouver-based business-to-business platform for comparing and booking shipments, provides a useful example of this type of service (Freightera, n.d.-a).]]

[24] p6 x72: ## 2021: Recovery Exposed Supply-chain Weaknesses

[25] p6 x108: [[Freight demand recovered as commercial activity resumed after the initial COVID-19 restrictions. Transport Canada (2022) documented higher port container traffic and increased truck crossings compared with 2020, reflecting the return of trade flows. Yet the transportation network remained vulnerable: container scarcity, overseas shipping delays, wildfires, and severe flooding in British Columbia disrupted key routes. End to end container transit from Shanghai to Toronto via West Coast ports averaged 34.5 days, well above the 2019 level of 25.9 days reported in Transport Canada’s historical series (Transport Canada, 2026).]]

[26] p7 x72: ## 2022: Strong Market Value, Persistent Congestion

[27] p7 x108: [[The market estimate rose to USD 105 billion, its strongest annual growth in the period (Ken Research, n.d.). Export demand for Canadian commodities benefited from upheaval in world supply markets, while imported goods continued to put pressure on terminals and inland connections (Transport Canada, 2023).]] The average Shanghai - Toronto container journey reached 39.9 days in 2022, the longest of the five years reviewed (Transport Canada, 2026). [[Freight prices and capacity constraints contributed to the higher market value, but neither should be interpreted as proof of better service or higher physical volumes.]]

[28] p7 x72: ## 2023: Weaker Goods Demand and Labour Disruption

[29] p7 x108: [[Growth slowed to 2.9% as inflation, higher financing costs, and softer spending reduced demand for some imported and industrial goods (Ken Research, n.d.; Transport Canada, 2024). The July 2023 labour dispute at British Columbia ports was especially disruptive. Statistics Canada (2023) reported that, relative to June, merchandise imports cleared through those ports fell 18.5% and exports departing them fell 23.0%; the figures were not seasonally adjusted. The Shanghai - Toronto transit measure improved to 35.0 days, though part of that relief reflected reduced congestion as demand eased (Transport Canada, 2026).]]

[30] p7 x72: ## 2024: Recovery Differed by Corridor

[31] p7 x108: [[The market estimate reached USD 111 billion, up 2.8% (Ken Research, n.d.). Freight volumes recovered in several transport modes, but results varied across Canadian gateways. Transport Canada (2025a) reported higher container activity at major ports alongside continued interruptions from labour disputes and extreme weather. Online retail remained a meaningful source of distribution demand: Statistics Canada (2026a) measured CAD 73.7 billion in e-commerce operating revenue for 2024, 9.0% above 2023.]]

[32] p8 x108: ## 2025: Trade Reorientation Reshaped Freight Flows

[33] p8 x108: [[The market estimate edged up to USD 112 billion, an increase of just 0.9% (Ken Research, n.d.). Transport Canada (2026) found that Canadian merchandise exports to the United States fell about 5%, while exports to non U.S. markets climbed roughly 16%. Truck entries into Canada decreased by about 5% and U.S. linked rail freight weakened, but marine gateways gained from overseas trade. The Port of Vancouver handled a record 170.4 million metric tonnes, 8% above 2024. Container transit from Shanghai to Toronto improved to 33.3 days, although it remained noticeably slower than in 2019 (Transport Canada, 2026).]]

[34] p8 x72: ## Interpretation of the Historical Trend

[35] p8 x108: [[The period began with a demand rebound that exceeded parts of the network’s practical capacity. As shipping conditions normalized, freight activity weakened in some routes and service reliability gradually improved. By 2025, trade policy was changing the geography of freight demand: transborder trucking faced pressure while ocean oriented corridors gained business. For employers, this history makes shipment visibility, contingency planning, customer communication, and adaptable routing more valuable than a narrow focus on shipment growth alone.]]
