Research and Analysis
Target Industry: Canadian Logistics
The project focuses on logistics in Canada, with particular interest in freight
transportation and the digital services that help firms book and coordinate shipments. Innovation,
Science and Economic Development Canada (2018) defines logistics as the planning,
implementation, and control of the movement and storage of goods, services, and related
information. In business practice, these activities connect suppliers, production sites,
warehouses, carriers, and customers. Freight transportation is central to that process, but reliable
distribution also depends on inventory control, order processing, and the exchange of accurate
information.
The industry is closely tied to Canada’s domestic economy and international trade.
Transport Canada (2025b) reports that transportation and warehousing contributed CAD 96.5
billion, or 4.3% of national GDP, in 2024. International merchandise trade was approximately
CAD 1.55 trillion that year, by value road transportation carried 46%, marine transport 21.1%,
air 11.6%, and rail 10.5%. These figures describe the transportation and warehousing sector and
merchandise flows, respectively.
Mordor Intelligence (2026) estimates that freight transportation accounted for 60.88% of
the Canadian freight and logistics market in 2025. That scale, combined with the growing use of
digital freight marketplaces, makes this field a relevant starting point for examining employers
and the qualifications they seek. Freightera, a Vancouver-based business-to-business platform for
comparing and booking shipments, provides a useful example of this type of service (Freightera,
n.d.-a).

2021: Recovery Exposed Supply-chain Weaknesses
Freight demand recovered as commercial activity resumed after the initial COVID-19
restrictions. Transport Canada (2022) documented higher port container traffic and increased
truck crossings compared with 2020, reflecting the return of trade flows. Yet the transportation
network remained vulnerable: container scarcity, overseas shipping delays, wildfires, and severe
flooding in British Columbia disrupted key routes. End to end container transit from Shanghai to
Toronto via West Coast ports averaged 34.5 days, well above the 2019 level of 25.9 days
reported in Transport Canada’s historical series (Transport Canada, 2026).
5
2022: Strong Market Value, Persistent Congestion
The market estimate rose to USD 105 billion, its strongest annual growth in the period
(Ken Research, n.d.). Export demand for Canadian commodities benefited from upheaval in
world supply markets, while imported goods continued to put pressure on terminals and inland
connections (Transport Canada, 2023). The average Shanghai - Toronto container journey
reached 39.9 days in 2022, the longest of the five years reviewed (Transport Canada, 2026).
Freight prices and capacity constraints contributed to the higher market value, but neither should
be interpreted as proof of better service or higher physical volumes.
2023: Weaker Goods Demand and Labour Disruption
Growth slowed to 2.9% as inflation, higher financing costs, and softer spending reduced
demand for some imported and industrial goods (Ken Research, n.d.; Transport Canada, 2024).
The July 2023 labour dispute at British Columbia ports was especially disruptive. Statistics
Canada (2023) reported that, relative to June, merchandise imports cleared through those ports
fell 18.5% and exports departing them fell 23.0%; the figures were not seasonally adjusted. The
Shanghai - Toronto transit measure improved to 35.0 days, though part of that relief reflected
reduced congestion as demand eased (Transport Canada, 2026).
2024: Recovery Differed by Corridor
The market estimate reached USD 111 billion, up 2.8% (Ken Research, n.d.). Freight
volumes recovered in several transport modes, but results varied across Canadian gateways.
Transport Canada (2025a) reported higher container activity at major ports alongside continued
interruptions from labour disputes and extreme weather. Online retail remained a meaningful
source of distribution demand: Statistics Canada (2026a) measured CAD 73.7 billion in
e-commerce operating revenue for 2024, 9.0% above 2023.
6
2025: Trade Reorientation Reshaped Freight Flows
The market estimate edged up to USD 112 billion, an increase of just 0.9% (Ken
Research, n.d.). Transport Canada (2026) found that Canadian merchandise exports to the United
States fell about 5%, while exports to non U.S. markets climbed roughly 16%. Truck entries into
Canada decreased by about 5% and U.S. linked rail freight weakened, but marine gateways
gained from overseas trade. The Port of Vancouver handled a record 170.4 million metric tonnes,
8% above 2024. Container transit from Shanghai to Toronto improved to 33.3 days, although it
remained noticeably slower than in 2019 (Transport Canada, 2026).
Interpretation of the Historical Trend
The period began with a demand rebound that exceeded parts of the network’s practical
capacity. As shipping conditions normalized, freight activity weakened in some routes and
service reliability gradually improved. By 2025, trade policy was changing the geography of
freight demand: transborder trucking faced pressure while ocean oriented corridors gained
business. For employers, this history makes shipment visibility, contingency planning, customer
communication, and adaptable routing more valuable than a narrow focus on shipment growth
alone.
Current Market and Outlook, 2026–2031
Mordor Intelligence (2026) places the freight and logistics market at USD 111.66 billion
in 2025 and USD 116.63 billion in 2026. Its forecast reaches USD 145.05 billion by 2031,
implying a 4.45% compound annual growth rate.

Conditions during 2026 help explain both the opportunity and the risk. Statistics Canada
(2026b) reports that real GDP increased 0.8% in the second quarter, supported by exports,
spending, and investment. At the same time, the Bank of Canada (2026) projects 1.0% real GDP
growth for 2026 and warns of persistent energy-price pressures and uncertainty over U.S. trade
policy. Its October report forecasts annual inflation of 2.9% in 2026 and 3.1% in 2027. Higher
fuel and input costs could compress margins for road-freight operators even if the nominal value
of logistics services rises.
Online commerce is likely to sustain demand for fulfilment, warehousing, and parcel
delivery. Mordor Intelligence (2026) forecasts faster growth in courier, express, and parcel
services than in the overall market, consistent with the expansion of online retail documented by
Statistics Canada (2026a). Trade diversification also supports port, rail, and intermodal services,
8
particularly when exporters need alternatives to established U.S.-focused corridors (Transport
Canada, 2026).
Digital systems can help companies plan routes, track exceptions, and coordinate
shipments, but their value depends on workflow integration and employee capabilities. Freight
marketplaces such as Freightera already demonstrate how shippers can compare carriers and
manage documentation online (Freightera, n.d.-a). Meanwhile, environmental performance
remains material: transport was responsible for approximately 22% of Canada’s greenhouse gas
emissions in 2024, including both passenger and freight activity (Transport Canada, 2026). For
logistics providers, fuel efficiency, network utilization, and lower-emission modes could become
competitive considerations alongside price and service.
The outlook is therefore cautiously positive. Demand is expected to shift toward
specialized, data enabled services, although labour availability, energy costs, climate-related
disruption, and North American trade conditions may affect the pace of growth.
